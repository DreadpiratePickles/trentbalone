03 — Brand pillars

Five non-negotiables.

Trent's brand and product are governed by the same five principles. Every screen, every word, every cycle Trent runs should make at least one of these more obviously true.

P / 01

Always operating.

Cycles run on a schedule, not on whether you opened the app. The company keeps moving while you sleep, while you ship, while you live.

P / 02

Permission-first.

Autonomy with approvals is the default. Risky moves — money, sends, deploys, deletions — stop and ask. You hold the keys; Trent holds the queue.

P / 03

Whole-business.

Strategy, code, marketing, support, finance — one operating loop. Not an AI developer. Not a chat tool. A complete company runtime.

P / 04

Memory that compounds.

Every decision, doc, customer note, and metric stays. The company gets smarter every week — not just the model.

P / 05

Operator-grade.

A serious console for serious work. Audit logs, budgets, kill switches, observability. The opposite of a slick chat box.

P / 06

Report, don't perform.

No emoji parties. No hype copy. Trent shows what it did, what it'll do next, and what it needs from you. That's the whole show.

Voice principles

Direct > clever.

Short sentences. Active voice. Verbs do the work. If a metaphor doesn't earn its keep, cut it.

Use operator vocabulary.

cycle, queue, approval, ship, budget, deploy. Trent runs a company; speak the language.

Always close the loop.

Three beats: what we did, what's next, what we need from you. Every message ends in clarity, not a cliffhanger.

No hype, no apology.

No "🚀", no "we're so excited", no "we're sorry for the inconvenience". State the state. Hand back control.

"Trent's got it." is the heartbeat.

The most repeated phrase in the brand. Used after every confirmation, every queue acknowledgement, every "done." It's not a tagline — it's how the product replies.

brand asset.

Trent earns trust by behaving the same way every time. These are the rules that keep the brand looking — and sounding — like the same partner across every surface.

Below this size, use the monogram instead.

Colour rules.

R / 01

90 / 8 / 2.

~90% of any surface is ink, bone, and neutrals. ~8% is the active signal (pulse OR ember, never both at once for the same element). ~2% is critical alerts. Pulse and ember never share a button.

R / 02

Pulse = AI moved. Ember = you decide.

Mint indicates an action Trent took on its own. Copper indicates an action that is waiting on a human. This colour grammar is sacred — never swap them.

R / 03

Contrast: AA minimum, AAA on body.

Body text on obsidian is bone @ 90% opacity (≥ 12:1). Mist for meta only. Pulse text never used below 14 px.

R / 04

No gradients on type.

Gradients live in atmosphere — hero glows, surface depth. Never in type, icons, or logos. Type is solid colour, always.

Writing rules.

W / 01

Lowercase the brand.

trent in product UI, system messages, and Trent's own voice. Trent capitalised in editorial copy, headlines, third-person mentions.

W / 02

Numbers as numerals.

9 agents, 3 PRs, 06:42. Trent is an operating system; spelled-out numbers feel like marketing copy.

W / 03

Oxford comma. Sentence case.

Titles, buttons, navigation — all sentence case. Title case feels corporate. Trent is closer to a journal than a brochure.

The team. In one hire.

Nine specialists. Each one is a role, not a model. Trent assigns the right agent to the right loop, runs them on a 24/7 cycle, and remembers what worked. Every job covered — nothing falls between people, because there is one of them.

PM · Product Manager

Prioritises & ships specs.

Owns the backlog, writes the specs, closes the loops. Knows which task moves the needle this week. Allergic to busywork.

EG · Engineer

Writes, reviews, merges.

Reads the repo, drafts the patch, opens the PR, runs the tests, waits for your nod. Production-quality code, every cycle.

GR · Growth

Experiments & doubles down.

Runs the funnel — landing pages, ads, channel tests, attribution. Stops at every spend gate. Reports what actually worked.

FN · Finance

Burn, runway, anomalies.

Tracks every dollar — token cost, ad spend, infra burn. Forecasts runway. Triggers caps. Flags anything that looks off.

MK · Marketing

Drafts, schedules, monitors.

Writes the copy, schedules the posts, watches the brand mentions. Tone-matched. Send-on-approve. Never auto-publishes.

OT · Ops

Tooling & vendor health.

Keeps the stack running. Watches integrations. Manages vendor relationships. The agent that fixes things before you notice.

CS · Customer Success

Reads, replies, escalates.

Reads every ticket. Drafts the warm, factual reply. Escalates anything with feeling, money, or risk straight to you.

LG · Legal

Reviews & flags risk.

Reads contracts, terms, vendor agreements. Flags risk in plain English. Keeps you compliant without keeping you waiting.

ST · Strategy

The Sunday letter.

Weekly synthesis. Pattern recognition across every other agent's cycle. Writes you a letter, in prose, every Sunday.

Signature product moments.

M / 01

One decision today.

Every morning, Trent surfaces the single highest-leverage decision waiting on you. Nothing else competes for that slot. It's the front page of the company.

M / 02

Cycle replay.

Scrub through any past 24-hour cycle like a timeline. See exactly which agent did what, which file changed, which email almost got sent. The audit log made beautiful.

M / 03

The Sunday letter.

Each Sunday Trent writes you a short letter: what shipped, what's stuck, what it learned about your company. Not a dashboard — prose. From your other founder.

M / 04

Kill switch as a feature.

A single keystroke pauses every agent on every company. The opposite of friction. Trent's trustworthiness is measured by how visible this control is.

M / 05

Public company page.

Every Trent company gets a public dashboard at [company].trent.app — what's been built, shipped, learned. Optional. Defaults to off.

M / 06

Memory search.

One bar searches every doc, decision, customer conversation, agent transcript, and metric your company has ever made. Trent is your company's other brain.

10 — Landing page · proposed redesign

From "AI cofounder"
to "the one hire."

The current landing leads with "Your AI cofounder. Runs the company." — accurate, but abstract. Most visitors don't know what an AI cofounder is. Everyone knows what an employee is, and everyone knows what it costs to hire one. Reframe the value through what the visitor already understands.

Strategy → Keep the operator-console aesthetic. Replace the abstract positioning band by band with the concrete one-employee frame. The agents and console mockup already do the work — the copy just needs to catch up.

The hero — rewrite.

Current · ed.01
"Your AI cofounder.
Runs the company.
You make one decision a day."
Accurate, but two leaps away from the value. The reader has to translate "AI cofounder" → "what does that do" → "what would I pay for that."
Proposed · ed.02
The one hire who does it all.
Nine specialists. One subscription. Always on the queue.
Leads with a category the reader knows (an employee, a hire) and a benefit (everything covered). Keeps "you make one decision a day" as the sub-paragraph below.

The manifesto quote — rewrite.

Current
"The best founders don't do everything. They decide what matters, then let their company run."
Aspirational, but doesn't differentiate from any productivity tool.
Proposed
"You used to need nine people to run a company. Now you need one."
Specific. Quantified. Anchors the new positioning before the reader scrolls into the agent roster.

Agents section header — rewrite.

Good. Just needs a sharper claim above it.
Proposed
9 specialists.
1 subscription.
0 hiring loops.

New sections to add.

R / 01

Insert "The Hire" band — right after the hero.

Add a full-width band between the marquee ticker and the manifesto. This is the new positioning's home. Two halves: on the left, the line "Hire a team. Or hire trent." R / 02

Insert "Needs doing?" interactive band — replacing the FeaturesSection grid.

The current six-card features grid (continuous cycles, approval queue, persistent memory, budget controls, weekly letters, integrations) describes mechanism. Replace it with a needs-grid that describes outcomes — nine real-sounding asks paired with the agent who handles them ("Need a contract reviewed?" → LG, "Need this PR shipped?" → EG, "Need our runway?" → FN). The mechanism cards can move below as a smaller secondary band.

  • Each card ends with the agent chip + a one-sentence answer.
  • Add a closing line in big serif: "Whatever it is — Trent's got it."
R / 03

Reframe the marquee ticker.

The current ticker reads like technical claims (autonomous execution · approval-first · budget-aware · 9 agents · zero ops tax · …). Mix in verbs the user owns — the things Trent is doing while they read:

  • shipping your PR · drafting your reply · reading your contracts · watching your burn · planning your week · catching your bugs · scheduling your posts · briefing your Sunday letter · staffing your team
  • Stays under the hero. Same visual treatment. Just more vivid copy.
R / 04

Add a "Sunday letter" preview band.

Above the CTA, add a stylised mockup of an actual Sunday letter — the brand's most distinctive deliverable. Three short paragraphs in --serif on bone-coloured paper texture inside the dark page. Signed "— trent · ST agent · sunday". This single section pivots the brand from "tool" to "partner who writes you letters" — exactly the cofounder moment we want to land before the CTA.

R / 05

Replace the stats row.

The current stats (9 agents · 24h · 1 decision · 142 cycles) are fine but inward-facing. Trade them for stats that frame Trent against the alternative the visitor would otherwise choose:

  • 0 · employees you onboard
  • 9 · roles staffed in one subscription
  • 24/7 · always on the queue
R / 06

Nav copy & CTA buttons.

Small but high-touch surfaces. Tighten the language to the new voice:

  • Top-nav: agents · the hire · needs · letters · pricing (the new "the hire" link anchors R/01).
  • Primary CTA: hire trent → (consider testing alongside the existing launch your console → — "hire trent" pulls the new positioning all the way through the funnel).
  • Footer line: trent · the one hire (replaces trent · cofounder OS).
R / 07

The micro-moments.

Three tiny surface changes that compound the new brand voice across the product:

  • Every empty state in the console ends with "Trent's got it." as a quiet reassurance.
  • The "one decision today" approval card uses the line "Trent ran the rest." as its footer caption.
  • The signed-in dashboard header reads "good morning, [name] — trent's been working." with a count of cycles run while they slept.
