# Change-order language, by trade

The sentence that keeps a job from turning into an argument. Use the owner's words when they
have them; these are the defaults.

## Contractors and home services

- On the quote: "Work outside this scope is priced separately in writing before it starts.
  Nothing is added to the invoice that was not signed off."
- When something is found on the job (soft subfloor, hidden pipe, rot behind a fascia): stop,
  photograph it, write the change order the same day: what was found, what it takes to fix,
  the price by the same rates as the quote, and one line asking for a "yes" by text or email.
- The change order carries the date and the way it was approved; the invoice repeats both.
- Never absorb an extra silently to avoid the conversation. It trains the customer to expect
  it, and it comes out of the owner's margin.

## Spa, salon, clinic

- On the quote or price list: "Add-on treatments are offered on the day and charged only if
  chosen."
- A package quote lists what is in the package; anything offered in the room is a separate
  line the customer said yes to, and the receipt shows it as its own line.
- A course of treatments states the number, the interval and what happens to unused sessions.

## Events and group bookings

- A headcount change after the confirmation date is priced in writing before the day.
- Minimum numbers, the deposit, and the date the final count is due are on the quote.

## Words to avoid

- "Extras may apply": says nothing and reads as a warning.
- "Subject to change": a price that is subject to change is not a quote.
- "TBD": name what is undecided and who decides it.
